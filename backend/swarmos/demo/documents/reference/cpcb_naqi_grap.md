# National Air Quality Index (NAQI) and Graded Response Action Plan — Reference Summary

> Summary written by SwarmOS for orientation. It is not an official document; refer to the Central Pollution Control Board (https://cpcb.nic.in) and the Commission for Air Quality Management (https://caqm.nic.in) for the authoritative text.

## NAQI categories
The National Air Quality Index, launched by CPCB in 2014, reports one number per location from up to eight pollutants (PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, NH3 and Pb). The overall index is the highest sub-index, and at least three pollutants, one of them PM2.5 or PM10, are needed. Categories: Good (0–50), Satisfactory (51–100), Moderately polluted (101–200), Poor (201–300), Very poor (301–400) and Severe (401–500).

## PM2.5 breakpoints (24-hour average, µg/m³)
0–30 Good, 31–60 Satisfactory, 61–90 Moderately polluted, 91–120 Poor, 121–250 Very poor, above 250 Severe.

## Health messaging
Poor air causes breathing discomfort on prolonged exposure; very poor air can cause respiratory illness on prolonged exposure; severe air affects healthy people and seriously affects people with existing disease. Advisories usually ask sensitive groups (children, older adults, people with heart or lung disease) to reduce outdoor exertion first.

## Graded Response Action Plan (Delhi-NCR)
GRAP is a staged set of measures applied in Delhi-NCR as the AQI worsens: Stage I at Poor, Stage II at Very poor, Stage III at Severe (401–450) and Stage IV at Severe+ (above 450). Typical measures escalate from dust control at construction sites, mechanised road sweeping and water sprinkling, and enforcement against open burning, to restrictions on non-essential construction and demolition, on older polluting vehicles and on truck entry, with schools and offices moving to remote or staggered work in the highest stage. Other cities often adapt similar staged plans; check the local plan before recommending measures.
