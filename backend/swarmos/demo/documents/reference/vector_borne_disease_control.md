# Vector-Borne Disease Control in Cities — Reference Summary

> Summary written by SwarmOS for orientation. It is not an official document; follow the guidelines of the National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control (https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in) and the local health department.

## Seasonality
Dengue, chikungunya and malaria cases in Indian cities usually rise during and just after the monsoon, when stagnant water provides breeding sites for Aedes and Anopheles mosquitoes.

## Source reduction
The most effective control for Aedes is removing breeding sites: emptying, covering or treating water containers, coolers, tyres, construction-site pits and blocked roof gutters. Many cities run a weekly "dry day" campaign and inspect construction sites, where water often collects.

## Surveillance
Health departments use larval indices from house-to-house surveys (such as the house index and Breteau index) together with reported fever and confirmed cases by ward to target fogging, larviciding and awareness drives.

## Links to city operations
Waterlogging complaints that stay unresolved for days, blocked drains and garbage accumulation increase breeding risk. Coordinating drainage and solid waste responses with the health department reduces outbreaks.
