# IMD Rainfall Intensity Classes and Heat Wave Criteria — Reference Summary

> Summary written by SwarmOS for orientation. It is not an official document; always check the current India Meteorological Department (IMD) publications at https://mausam.imd.gov.in before acting.

## Rainfall intensity (24-hour accumulation at a station)
IMD describes 24-hour rainfall as very light (0.1–2.4 mm), light (2.5–15.5 mm), moderate (15.6–64.4 mm), heavy (64.5–115.5 mm), very heavy (115.6–204.4 mm) and extremely heavy (204.5 mm or more). Urban drainage problems usually begin at heavy rainfall, and waterlogging becomes widespread at very heavy rainfall, especially where drains are silted or encroached.

## Heat wave criteria
For plains stations, a heat wave is considered when the maximum temperature is at least 40 °C and is 4.5 °C to 6.4 °C above normal; a severe heat wave when it is 6.5 °C or more above normal. Independently of the normal, a maximum of 45 °C or more is treated as a heat wave and 47 °C or more as a severe heat wave. Coastal stations use a threshold of 37 °C with the same departures, and hill stations 30 °C.

## Warm nights
IMD also reports warm nights when the minimum temperature stays well above normal while the maximum is at least 40 °C. Warm nights increase health risk because the body cannot recover overnight.

## Colour-coded impact-based warnings
IMD district warnings use green (no action), yellow (be updated), orange (be prepared) and red (take action). City operations plans commonly link standby and pre-positioning decisions to orange and red warnings.
