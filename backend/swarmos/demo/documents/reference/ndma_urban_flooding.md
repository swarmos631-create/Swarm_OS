# Urban Flood Management — Reference Summary of NDMA Guidance

> Summary written by SwarmOS for orientation. It is not an official document; see the National Disaster Management Authority's Guidelines on Management of Urban Flooding (2010) at https://ndma.gov.in for the authoritative text.

## Why cities flood
Urban flooding is driven by intense short-duration rainfall falling on large paved areas, storm water drains that are undersized, silted or encroached, solid waste blocking drains and culverts, construction on natural drainage channels and lakes, and backflow when outfalls are submerged by high river or tide levels.

## Preparedness
NDMA guidance emphasises a city-level flood plan with ward-level responsibilities; mapping of waterlogging-prone locations and low-lying areas; desilting of primary, secondary and tertiary drains well before the monsoon; removal of encroachments on drains and water bodies; inventory and testing of dewatering pumps; and rainwater harvesting and recharge to reduce runoff.

## Early warning and monitoring
Cities are encouraged to use local rain gauges and Doppler weather radar products, link rainfall forecasts to drainage capacity, and share alerts with ward engineers, traffic police and emergency services. Complaint hotspots from citizen reports are a useful near-real-time signal of blocked drains.

## Response
During heavy rain the priorities are keeping underpasses, hospital access roads and arterial routes open, deploying pumps to known waterlogging points, isolating electrical hazards such as fallen wires in water, and coordinating diversions with traffic police. After the event, drains and culverts that failed should be inspected and recorded so that capital works can be prioritised.
