# Drinking Water Quality (IS 10500:2012) and Supply Norms — Reference Summary

> Summary written by SwarmOS for orientation. It is not an official document; consult the Bureau of Indian Standards (https://www.bis.gov.in) for IS 10500 and the CPHEEO Manual on Water Supply and Treatment for design norms.

## Key IS 10500:2012 limits
The standard gives an acceptable limit and, for some parameters, a higher permissible limit that applies only when no alternative source exists. Examples: pH 6.5–8.5 (no relaxation); turbidity 1 NTU acceptable, 5 NTU permissible; total dissolved solids 500 mg/L acceptable, 2000 mg/L permissible; free residual chlorine at least 0.2 mg/L where water is chlorinated; nitrate 45 mg/L; fluoride 1.0 mg/L acceptable, 1.5 mg/L permissible. E. coli and total coliform bacteria must not be detectable in any 100 mL sample.

## What exceedances usually mean
Low residual chlorine or detected coliforms suggest contamination in the distribution network (for example through leaking pipes during intermittent supply) and call for flushing, re-chlorination and sampling upstream. High turbidity often follows heavy rain or treatment problems.

## Supply norms
CPHEEO design norms commonly cited for urban water supply are 135 litres per capita per day for cities with a piped sewerage system and 150 litres per capita per day for metropolitan cities, plus an allowance for losses. Non-revenue water from leaks is a major gap in many Indian cities; minimum night flow analysis by district metered area (DMA) is a standard way to locate leakage.
